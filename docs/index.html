The integration tax

Accepting MUSD used to mean building a payments team.

Before a merchant can take one dollar of Bitcoin-backed stablecoin, they need wallet plumbing, chain monitoring and delivery guarantees. Paykit deletes that list.

What you'd build yourself

  • 01Wire a wallet connector and chain switching
  • 02Encode ERC-20 transfer() calldata
  • 03Poll Mezo RPC for Transfer logs
  • 04Match amounts to orders, expire stale ones
  • 05Run a server and deliver webhooks
  • 06Design a checkout modal people trust

What you write with Paykit

The Worker handles intents, chain watching and webhooks. You keep your MUSD. Paykit never holds funds: payments go straight from buyer to merchant.

How a payment moves

From click to confirmed in five hops.

Nothing custodial, nothing to deploy. The Worker reads Mezo with eth_getLogs and only ever writes to your webhook.

Hop 1 · Browser

Widget renders

The tag reads its data-* attributes and draws a Pay button. No build step.

Hop 2 · Worker

Intent opens

A 15-minute payment intent is stored in Cloudflare KV with the amount and merchant.

Hop 3 · Mezo

Buyer pays

MetaMask signs an MUSD transfer() on chain 31611, or the buyer sends manually.

Hop 4 · Worker

Chain is read

Each status poll scans MUSD Transfer logs to the merchant since the last block seen.

Hop 5 · You

You get paid

Status flips to completed, paykit:success fires, your webhook gets the tx hash.

data-amount
Price in MUSD. Decimals allowed, 18-decimal precision on chain.

        
data-merchant
Your Mezo address. MUSD goes here directly, never through Paykit.

        
data-token
MUSD contract. Testnet 0x1189…c503, mainnet 0xdD46…F186.

        
data-endpoint
Your Paykit Worker URL. Self-host it or use the hosted one.

        
paykit:success
Window event with detail.tx_hash once the transfer is confirmed.
